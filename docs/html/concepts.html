Concepts

Slides, logical coordinates, the zooming camera, and how a deck is recorded and replayed.

Slides and the forest#

A presentation is a forest of slides, most often a single tree. In Markdown a slide is a ::: slide container; in code it is an instance of Slide. Every slide owns a logical canvas, 16 × 9 units by default, and its content is authored in those units regardless of where the slide ends up on screen.

A child slide is placed in its parent through a viewport: a rectangle in the parent's canvas that shows the child's whole canvas, scaled uniformly, possibly rotated and letterboxed. Because viewports nest, the transform of a slide is the composition of the transforms of its ancestors, and when a viewport moves every descendant follows.

root (32 × 18)
├── chapter A (16 × 9, viewport at x=-8, width 12)
│   ├── detail A1
│   └── detail A2 (rotated by 0.1 rad)
└── chapter B

Pre-order visits#

Playback walks each tree in pre-order, and cuts from the last stop of one root to the next root: a slide runs its own steps and visits its children in order, each visit zooming the camera into the child's viewport; from a finished child the camera goes straight to the next sibling, and zooms back out to the parent after the last one. The order within a slide is the source order of its includes, unless an include carries step=N, which interleaves the visit with the parent's numbered fragments. A child's content is invisible until its first visit and fades in then, unless its include says preview=true or presenter.revealOnEntry is false. return_to_parent, skip_return and skip_next change where the camera goes when a child ends. A deck whose slides include none of the others is a forest of single slides, a flat document read page after page, as a PDF opened in the player is.

Coordinates#

Inside a slide, coordinates are the slide's own: the origin is the center of the canvas, x grows to the right and y upwards, and one unit is one unit of the canvas whatever the zoom level. Text is positioned by its left edge and first baseline, images and shapes by their center, and any element can choose another anchor. Children are placed by the center of their viewport.

Stops, clips and recording#

When a deck loads, the complete slide program runs once, instantly. Every stop (a click) closes a clip that remembers the animations that produced it, and the player stores a compact delta snapshot of the settled state after each clip. Navigation restores a snapshot and replays the clip's animations in real time, so:

  • forward and backward steps, outline jumps and deep links are immediate;
  • the left key can play the current stop backwards, because clips are made of interpolations;
  • author code runs exactly once per page load, like a single render.

Visibility follows the forest: only the first root is visible at the start, each descendant fades in on its first entry and stays visible afterwards, so parents show their finished children as previews.
