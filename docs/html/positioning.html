Positioning

Anchors, flow, explicit coordinates, relative constraints, at= expressions, offsets and sizes.

Open in the guide ↗

Anchors#

x, y position an element's anchor. The default anchor is left-baseline for prose, headings, math and listings (the left edge and the first baseline) and center for images, shapes, listings' siblings and child viewports. anchor= chooses another point:

Anchors Available on
center, top, bottom, left, right, top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right every element and viewport
left-baseline, right-baseline text, math and listings
origin groups (their local origin) 
![](assets/tiles.png){#img x=-9.35 y=0.4 width=4 anchor=top-left}
[[rectangle: #r x=9.35 y=-5 width=3 height=1.2 anchor=bottom-right]]
[Ends at x = 9.35]{#rb x=9.35 y=-0.4 anchor=right-baseline}

layout.textAnchor changes the default for text.

Flow#

Blocks without coordinates flow downward from the top-left margin of the canvas, separated by layout.gap. Text with only x keeps flowing at that left edge; an explicit y, an at= or a vertical constraint leaves the flow. Elements that leave the flow do not reserve space, so later blocks move up. A center-anchored element with one missing coordinate defaults that axis to zero.

Relative constraints#

Open in the guide ↗

### A heading {#h x=0.6 y=1.6}

[Below it, left-aligned]{#p below:#h left-aligned:#h buff=0.3}

[A side note]{#note right-of:#p buff=0.5 font_size=18 color=GRAY}

[Big]{#big x=0.6 y=-1.4 font_size=48}
[shares the baseline]{x=2.3 baseline-aligned:#big}
  • below, above, left-of and right-of place the element beyond the reference's bounding edge, centered on the other axis, at distance buff (default layout.gap).
  • left-aligned, right-aligned, top-aligned and bottom-aligned match an edge without moving the other axis.
  • baseline-aligned matches the typographic baseline of text, math or listings.
  • One direction and at most one constraint per axis are allowed; an explicit coordinate may set the free axis (below:#h x=0).

References target elements of the same slide (and the same group), including later ones and hidden fragments, but never arrows, lines, braces or boxes. Sizes are measured once during preparation; dependency cycles are reported with their line.

Anchor expressions#

Open in the guide ↗

[[rectangle: #ref x=-6 y=-2.2 width=3 height=1.6]]
[My top-left at its bottom-right]{#tl at=top-left:#ref.bottom-right dx=0.2}
[[dot: #d at=#ref.top color=RED]]
[Centered on a point]{#cp at=center:(5,-3.6)}

at=[anchor:]#id[.anchor] or at=[anchor:](x,y) puts one anchor of the element (the prefix, or anchor=, or its default) on an anchor of another element (center when omitted) or on a literal point. It replaces x, y and the relative constraints, combines with dx/dy, and follows the same reference rules. Inside directives, keep (x,y) tuples together; the comma inside parentheses does not split attributes.

Offsets and sizes#

  • dx, dy shift the final position of any positioned element.
  • width controls paragraph wrapping, image width and the maximum width of formulas and listings; height scales the result; font_size overrides the layout size.
  • opacity (0 to 1) makes any element translucent; fades preserve it.

Children#

Includes and imports take the same attributes against local elements: x, y, anchor, at=, the relative constraints, dx/dy and .class styles. Their width/height size the viewport, not the child's canvas.
