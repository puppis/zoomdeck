PDFs, flat decks and forests

A PDF opened as it is, a Markdown file of plain slides read page after page, and the forest of slides that every deck is, each shown live.

The simplest deck is one you already have. This page goes from a PDF shown as it is, through a Markdown file that lists plain slides, to the forest of slides that every deck is. Every deck below is the real player: click one to give it the keys, then press → and ←.

A PDF#

Zoomdeck opens a PDF directly: a paper, a poster, slides typeset elsewhere. Each page becomes a slide, the pages are visited first to last, and the player cuts from one to the next with no zoom and no fade, as a document is read. The bookmarks of the document, where it has them, name the pages in the outline; the others are numbered. Here, three pages typeset with LaTeX:

zoomdeck serve paper.pdf                       # present it
zoomdeck build paper.pdf -o out/paper --one-file   # a page that plays it, file embedded

The command, the development page (/?deck=path/to/paper.pdf), the desktop app and mountDeck in a page of your own all take a PDF where they take a deck. The presentation viewport takes the proportions of the first page, so a portrait paper is shown upright with room at the sides and landscape slides fill the screen; the title is the document's own, or its file name. Pages are drawn by pdf.js at the density of the screen wherever the camera rests, drawn again when the window changes size, and reduced to a small picture once out of view, so the text of a page stays as sharp as the deck's own while a long document never holds every page at full resolution. The pen, the whiteboard, the spotlight, recording and printing work on a PDF as on any deck. The exported folder copies the file next to the page; the single-file export embeds it.

A Markdown file of plain slides#

A Markdown file may simply list slides, one ::: slide container after another, including none of them. They are then visited in the order they are written, with a cut from each to the next, like the pages of the PDF above, and the deck ends on the last one. Each slide is an ordinary slide otherwise: it has steps, fragments and animations, as the second one here, and it may include children of its own, which are entered and left with the usual zooms, as the third one does.

A slide that would rather pan to its neighbour says transition=zoom; the slides then glide past one another, since they are laid out side by side. The same file is at examples/document/pages.md in the repository.

Forests and trees#

Both cases are instances of one rule: a deck is a forest of slides. The slides that nothing includes are its roots, visited first to last with cuts between them; an include places a child inside a slide, and the camera zooms into children and back out. A PDF is a forest of single pages, a file of plain slides a forest of single slides, and a talk with one slide that includes all the others is a single tree, the most common deck of all. Here, a forest of two trees: a tree of two children, then a second root.

The outline lists the roots at the top level and the children under them. root in deck.json names the single root of a tree; naming one in a deck with several roots is an error. Slides and the forest has the includes, the visit order and the flags that shape a tree.
