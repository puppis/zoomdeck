A quick tour

The main things Zoomdeck does, each shown live on this page with the markup that produces it. Click a deck to give it the keys, then use the arrows.

The same tour as a deck: open the tour ↗

Every deck on this page is the real player, embedded in the page. Click one to give it the keyboard, press → and ← to move, or use its buttons. The markup beside a deck is all it takes.

A PDF, as it is#

This chapter in the tour ↗

Zoomdeck opens a PDF directly: a paper, a poster, slides typeset elsewhere. Each page is a slide, the pages are visited first to last with a cut between them, the bookmarks name them in the outline, and the text stays sharp at any zoom because pages are drawn at the density of the screen. The pen, the spotlight, recording and printing work on a PDF as on any deck.

zoomdeck serve paper.pdf          # present it
zoomdeck build paper.pdf -o out   # export a page that plays it

Plain slides, in a row#

This chapter in the tour ↗

A deck of your own is a Markdown file of ::: slide containers. Slides that include no other slide are simply visited in order, with cuts and no animation, like the pages of a document.

Slides as a tree#

This chapter in the tour ↗

An [[include: #id …]] places one slide inside another, as a viewport with a position and a size. The camera zooms into each child in turn, goes from a child straight to its next sibling, and zooms back out after the last one. Here a root has two children and the second child has a child of its own: first child, next sibling, then down again.

Spotlight and laser#

This chapter in the tour ↗

Hold Shift while moving the pointer to dim everything but a circle around it; the L key (or the Pointer setting) turns the spotlight into a red laser dot, Shift + scroll resizes either, and Shift + drag draws a highlight. Both work on any deck, including a PDF, and are recorded with a talk. Try it on the deck below.

Pen, tablet and whiteboard#

This chapter in the tour ↗

D turns the pen on: a drag draws on the slide in the chosen color (keys 1 to 4), with pressure from a pen or an Apple Pencil, and the strokes stay with the slide. A tablet or an iPad pairs with the player as a drawing pad (Settings → Tablet), so you write on it and the audience sees the slide: pressure, palm rejection, erasing with a finger. W opens the whiteboard, an infinite canvas of its own that keeps what you wrote and comes back when you need it again.

Camera, recording and video#

This chapter in the tour ↗

The camera (C) shows you next to or over the slides, as a picture in a corner or full screen. A recording (R) keeps the whole talk: your voice, the camera and every move on the deck, strokes included; it plays back on the deck itself, where it stays navigable, and exports as a plain video file. A shared screen (S), another application say, can be filmed as part of the talk. The desktop app is to render a recording off line as well, faster than real time, into one flat video file. The deck below has these controls on; each asks the browser for permission the first time.

Formulas, diagrams and animation#

This chapter in the tour ↗

Formulas are LaTeX, inline or displayed, rendered by MathJax and zoomable like everything else. Diagrams are directives: arrows between elements, boxes and blobs around them, icons, braces, and any of them hand-drawn with sketch=true. Animations in the style of Manim come with the same directives: elements move, grow, change colour, morph into one another, get highlighted, or are written stroke by stroke in the bundled handwriting face, one step per click. The deck below has three slides: a formula, a diagram, and five steps of motion.
