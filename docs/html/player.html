The player

Keyboard and pointer controls, the pen and its pressure styles, the whiteboard, a companion tablet, spotlight and laser, reverse playback, deep links, speed and fullscreen.

Open in the guide ↗

Controls#

Default keys Action
→, Space next stop (plays its animations)
← previous stop, playing the current stop backwards
Home first stop
R replay the current stop
⌘Z / Ctrl+Z undo the last pen or highlighter stroke
⌘⇧Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z / Ctrl+Y redo a stroke
Delete, Backspace clear all strokes on this slide
L switch the pointer tool between spotlight and laser
W show or hide the whiteboard; while it is shown the arrows scroll it
F toggle fullscreen; Esc leaves it
O toggle the slide outline
, open the settings

The footer has previous/next buttons, a progress bar, the pen settings, the speed control (0.5× to 2×), the settings gear and the fullscreen button. The header shows the breadcrumb of the current slide and the player state.

Settings#

The gear button (or the , key) opens the settings dialog, in five parts:

  • Pointer: the tool shown by the pointer gesture (spotlight or laser) and the modifier keys of each gesture: the pen, the spotlight/highlighter and click-to-advance. Each can be "Plain" (no key), one of Shift, Control, Alt, ⌘/Win or a pair of them, or "Off"; choosing keys already used by another gesture frees them from it.
  • Pen: how pressure and motion shape a stroke; see The pen.
  • Tablet: pairing a companion tablet; see A tablet as a drawing pad.
  • Keyboard: every action with its keys. Click a key to remove it, or choose "Add key" and press the new combination, which may include modifiers; a combination already used elsewhere moves to the new action.
  • Advanced: the reverse-playback switch, on by default.

"Restore default gestures", "Restore the default pen" and "Restore default keys" undo the changes of their section. Settings are stored in the browser and apply to every deck you open there.

Pointer tools#

With the default gestures:

  • Drag draws with the pen; a click leaves a dot. Color and tip size are in the footer, where "Default" is the deck's text color.

  • Hold Shift for a spotlight that follows the pointer, or a red laser dot: the L key (or the Pointer setting) switches between the two. Shift + scroll resizes either; Shift + drag draws a highlighter stroke.

  • A click never moves between stops unless you ask for it: the "Click: next stop" gesture is off by default, and binding it to a key (for example Alt) in the settings makes that key + click behave like the → key.

  • Double-click a link that points inside the deck (#slide=, #step=) to follow it; a single click over such a link draws, since it often covers a whole tile. Links to pages outside the deck open on a single click.

Strokes belong to the current slide and clear when the slide changes; undo, redo and clear act on the current slide's strokes (or on the whiteboard's while it is shown). Links and live pages keep working under a plain pen: clicking one opens it rather than drawing, with no highlight while the pointer rests on it. On a touch screen a finger never draws, so a resting palm leaves no marks; drawing takes a pen, a stylus or the mouse. A fingertip rubbed over a stroke erases it, and undo brings it back where it was; two fingers scroll the whiteboard. The colors in the footer are swatches, the first being the deck's own text color.

The pen#

A stroke is a path of samples with pressure, drawn as a filled shape whose width varies along it, the way drawing applications render ink. A pen with a pressure sensor (Apple Pencil, a Wacom tablet, a pen-enabled laptop) shapes the line as you press; with a mouse or a basic stylus the width follows the speed of the hand instead, so a quick flick ends thin. Samples are taken from the browser's raw pointer stream, before the frame is painted, which keeps the line close to the tip.

The Pen section of the settings holds the look:

Setting Effect
Preset Ballpoint (light pressure effect), Fountain pen (strong, tapered), Marker (constant width), Brush (strongest); moving a slider makes it Custom
Pencil width width of a pencil stroke relative to the tip size, 0.2 to 1.5 (0.6 by default: a pencil draws finer than a mouse at the same tip); mouse strokes are not affected
Pencil pressure how much a real pressure sensor counts, 0.2 to 1.5 (0.6 by default, so a pencil draws about as light as a mouse); mouse strokes are not affected
Pressure effect how much pressure widens the line, from -1 (harder is thinner) to 1
Smoothing softening of the outline
Steadying how far the line lags behind the hand, which irons out jitter
Taper the ends thin the stroke in and out
Vary the width with speed pressure simulated from speed when the device reports none

Pencil strokes use half the steadying, since a pressure pen reports dense and precise positions, the line is extended with the browser's prediction of the pen's next positions so it stays under the tip, and fast strokes are drawn along a smooth curve through the samples, so quick handwriting keeps round curves. Changing a setting redraws the strokes already on screen, so you can tune the look against a line you just drew. Color and tip size stay in the footer; the tip size is the width at medium pressure. The highlighter always has a constant width.

The whiteboard#

W (or the Whiteboard button in the footer) covers the slides with a blank page in the deck's background color, dotted to show where it scrolls. Draw on it as on a slide. It is a single page without edges: the arrow keys glide the view by a quarter of its width or height, the mouse wheel or a two-finger scroll on a trackpad moves it directly, and on a touch screen two fingers drag it. Space still moves the presentation underneath, so W again returns to the slides where you left them.

The whiteboard keeps its content while you move through the deck, and its undo, redo and clear are its own: nothing done there touches the strokes on slides. Its content lasts until the page is reloaded.

A tablet as a drawing pad#

A tablet on the same network can show the current slide and let you draw on it with a pencil, mirroring the strokes on the presenting screen as they are drawn, in both directions. The tablet runs none of the engine: it shows a picture of the presenting screen's view, which the presenter sends again whenever a stop settles or the window is resized, and draws the strokes itself as vector ink on top. The page therefore loads at once on any tablet, answers the pencil immediately, and accepts no gesture other than drawing: a pencil or a finger never selects, scrolls or zooms anything.

  1. Serve the deck so that other devices can reach it: zoomdeck serve deck.json --host, or npm run dev -- --host from the checkout. Pairing needs this server; an exported HTML file opened from disk cannot pair.
  2. On the presenting computer, open the settings and, under Tablet, choose "Show the pairing code". A QR code and an address appear.
  3. Scan the code with the tablet (or type the address): the pad page opens there, with the current slide and a toolbar for the tool (pen or highlighter), color swatches, tip size, undo, redo, clear and the whiteboard, and nothing else. A fingertip erases the stroke it touches. Adding it to the home screen gives it the whole screen.

Over a cable. The pairing needs a network between the two devices, not necessarily Wi-Fi. On a Mac, plug the iPad in, open System Settings → General → Sharing → Internet Sharing, share your connection "from" the interface you use (Wi-Fi) "to" iPad USB, and turn it on; the computer gets an address on the cable (192.168.2.1 as a rule), the pairing panel shows a code for it next to the Wi-Fi one, and the tablet scans that one. The cable link is private and steadier than a crowded Wi-Fi. An iPad with a cellular plan can also share its own connection to the computer over USB (Personal Hotspot), which works the same way.

The pad follows the presentation and cannot move it, so a stray touch never changes the slide. Strokes, undo, redo, clear, and the whiteboard with its scrolling are shared both ways; each page draws the shared strokes with its own pen style, so the presenting screen always shows them in the style chosen there. An Apple Pencil hovering over the tablet shows as a red dot on the presenting screen. A graphics tablet without a screen (a Wacom) needs no pairing: plugged into the computer, it drives the pointer and draws with pressure directly.

The guide's chapter Pen, whiteboard and tablet is a test page for all of this: shapes to trace, a strip for the pressure, a box to write in with the palm resting, and the checklist to go through once the tablet is paired.

The pad page needs almost no memory, since the deck itself stays on the computer. Opening the deck itself on a tablet, as a viewer, is also possible when it is small enough: Safari closes a page that uses too much memory ("A problem repeatedly occurred"), so text is rasterized at the same base density on every screen and only the lines in view are sharpened to the screen's density.

Reverse playback#

Going back plays the stop's animations in reverse: fades, property animations, effects, morphs and camera moves retrace their path, including zooming back into a child you just left. Videos, loops and updater-driven waits cannot run backwards and are passed over. Pressing ← again during a reverse settles it and steps back once more, so quick presses step through stops. "presenter": { "reversePlayback": false } restores instant jumps.

Cuts#

"presenter": { "transition": "cut" } replaces the view at once when the camera moves between slides, like turning the pages of a PDF, while fragments and animations inside a slide still play. transition=cut on a slide, include or subslide does the same for that slide alone, and transition=zoom brings the zoom back for one slide of a cut deck.

Outline and deep links#

The outline lists every slide; clicking one jumps to its first stop. The address bar follows the current stop as #step=N, which can be shared or bookmarked; #slide=id opens the first stop of a slide by its markup ID (or a slide by name).

Loops and automatic advancement#

Looping stops (play=loop, stop({ loop: true })) repeat until the next click. Stops marked with next or autoNext advance by themselves once played; backward moves never trigger them.

Editing preview#

zoomdeck serve and the dev page reload when the deck's files change and return to the slide and step you were on, using a bookmark saved just before the reload. Parse and reference errors are shown with the offending line.

Static exports#

A deck can be exported as a static folder or as one self-contained HTML file that opens from disk; both keep the complete player, including settings and deep links. See Exporting a deck.
